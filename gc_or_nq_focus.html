The short answer

  • Gold (GC) is the better default screen. Over your whole day in 2022–2026, always watching gold collected 67.5 points per 100 half-hour rows; always watching Nasdaq (NQ) collected 57.1.
  • No switching rule fixed in advance did better than simply watching gold: the locked playbook 66.9, the clock alone 66.8, “the hotter one at 12:00 SGT (00:00 New York in summer, 23:00 in winter)” 64.4.
  • What held in both periods is the clock. From 12:00 SGT (00:00 New York in summer, 23:00 in winter) to the London open (15:00 SGT in summer, 16:00 SGT in winter; 03:00 New York) gold scored more (US summer clock: by 31.8 then 31.9; winter: by 29.5 then 33.5); most of that gap is Nasdaq rows too small to count. “Too small to count” is measured against each market’s own typical half hour of the whole trading day; Nasdaq’s movement is mostly in the US hours. Under the study’s rule 32.2 and 32.7 in 100 of Nasdaq’s Asia half hours and 14.9 and 11.9 of gold’s were too small (2015–2021, 2022–2026); against each market’s usual size for those same half hours 9.0 and 6.2 (Nasdaq) and 3.7 and 4.2 (gold). Judged that way, gold’s Asia lead over all clocks shrinks to 13.2 in 2015–2021 and is mostly gone in 2022–2026 (3.2 left); in the clock-change weeks of 2015–2021 it stayed (−30.2 on 94 days; −1.8 in 2022–2026), but those weeks are the least clean under this yardstick because the 20 earlier days straddle the clock change. That is a description read off after the results of both periods had already been seen (see “Why gold wins the Asia afternoon”).
  • In the half hour before the New York cash open gold scored more in both periods and both clocks: 21:00 SGT (09:00 New York) in summer, 22:00 SGT (09:00 New York) in winter.
  • From the cash open, 21:30 SGT (09:30 New York), to the end of your day Nasdaq scored more in the US summer clock (+7.4 then +8.0); in the US winter clock, from 22:30 SGT (09:30 New York), that lead did not hold (+8.9 then +0.1).
  • Day types, news days and volatility states gave no reason to switch: 54 situations tried, 6 passed the first look, 4 held, and none changes which market to watch.

What this study cannot tell you

Must not be concluded

  • That the playbook or any fixed rule beat always watching gold; or that the scores are trading results (they are labels of chart shape, +3 / +1 / −1; the hindsight bar is for scale only).
  • That a Nasdaq gap or a busy Nasdaq overnight turns the Asia afternoon into Nasdaq hours, or that a hard Nasdaq close means “switch”.
  • That the fragile one (gold busier overnight) holds, or that the shifts carry over in full to your own label settings (they shrink).
  • That Nasdaq leads in the cash session in the US winter clock (from 22:30 SGT (09:30 New York)) or in the clock-change weeks, or at 20:30 SGT (08:30 New York) in the summer clock.
  • That “gold everywhere, Nasdaq from 21:30 SGT (09:30 New York) in the US summer clock” is a tested rule: it was read off after the results of both periods had already been seen.

Limits

  • Both markets used gold’s strict holiday list, so some Nasdaq dates are left out of this study only (see “How this was done”).
  • Each market is judged against its own usual size: a half hour is too small to count when it moved less than half the market’s typical half hour of the whole trading day over the 20 days before. The study cannot say which moves more dollars; it says which chart more often had the shapes you trade.
  • “The hotter one at 12:00 SGT (00:00 New York in summer, 23:00 in winter)”: on days without that reading it counts the mean of the two.
  • Not tried: outside series (VIX, the dollar, bond yields; 10-year Treasury futures would add most), company earnings days, month of the year, high-volatility years as a class, and minute-by-minute attention rules (a setup close on one chart only).
  • Your two fixed-rule setups, traded per part of the day: not counted in this study (NOT VERIFIED).
  • Recent days: only 23 common days could be scored (the Nasdaq recent file is short, and the first weeks of each file are needed as warm-up); they cannot confirm or overturn anything. The recent Nasdaq file shares no day with the ten-year file, so its export is not cross-checked bar by bar.
  • Gold’s lead in the Asia afternoon over the ten years rested on about a third of Nasdaq’s Asia half hours being too small to count. That lead is conditional on Nasdaq staying quiet in Asia against its own whole-day size. These 23 days are too few, and come from a file whose export is not yet cross-checked, to call it a change.
  • The Asia result depends on the size yardstick. Judged against each market’s own typical half hour of the whole trading day (the study’s rule) gold leads the Asia afternoon by about 30 points per 100 half hours (score points, not price); judged against each market’s usual size for those same half hours the lead is 13.2 in 2015–2021 and 3.2 in 2022–2026. That second reading was made after the results of both periods had already been seen and is a description only.
  • Overnight, from 08:00 SGT (20:00 New York in summer, 19:00 in winter) to 16:00 SGT (04:00 New York in summer, 03:00 in winter), gold and Nasdaq moved together minute by minute much more in the recent files than in the same weeks of 2025: on a scale from 0 to 1, 0.34 and 0.22 against 0.03 and 0.06 (summer and winter clock). Not explained; it does not enter any score.

How this was done

Data: gold (GC) and Nasdaq (NQ) 1-minute bars, ten years each. A study day is a trading date present in both files: 1,434 days from 2015-12-14 to 2021-12-30 and 975 days from 2022-01-03 to 2026-04-24.

Days left out (source: the study notes): 105 Nasdaq dates that gold’s strict holiday list removes; 11 gold dates without Nasdaq (10 before Nasdaq’s file starts, 1 in 2016); 61 Nasdaq dates without gold (40 of them a gap in the gold file from 2025-05-28 to 2025-07-25).

Labels: your 5-minute chart (5MA, 20MA, Bollinger 20/2), half-hour rows from 12:00 SGT (00:00 New York in summer, 23:00 in winter) to 01:00 SGT (13:00 New York in summer, 12:00 in winter), Trend / Swing / Whipsaw at the study’s default settings; a half hour is compared only when both markets have a label.

The rule was fixed in advance and the 54 situations were frozen before any result existed. A situation had to move the score by 15 points or more, clearly more than its week-to-week wobble, in 2015–2021, and then again by 10 or more in 2022–2026. The wobble was measured by redrawing whole weeks 2,000 times (a bootstrap). 2022–2026 was opened once, after the 2015–2021 results and the playbook were locked (file fingerprints checked).

Checks: an independent recomputation of five situations matched; a line-by-line look-ahead audit (no sign reads a bar later than the moment it is read; tested by changing later bars on 40 real days); a review of both stages by a second checker; the final reading by the primary, who may lower a verdict but never raise one.
